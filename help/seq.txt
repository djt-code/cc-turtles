seq: print a sequence of numbers (as GNU's seq; not in POSIX)

  seq [-w] [-s sep] [-f format] [first [increment]] last

Prints the numbers from first (1) to last, increment (1) apart, one a line: `seq 3` is 1 2 3, `seq 0 5 20` 0 5 10 15 20, `seq 5 -1 1` counts down.  Decimals and negative numbers work (`seq -1 0.5 1`); the numbers are printed with as many decimal places as first or increment has. Nothing is printed if last comes before first.
  -s sep
      between the numbers instead of a newline (one still ends the line).  \t, \n and \\ in it are a tab, a newline and a backslash (GNU's seq leaves that to the shell).
  -w
      the same width for all: leading zeros (seq -w 8 10: 08 09 10)
  -f format
      each number through a printf floating-point format, such as %.2f, %g or %05.1f (one %e, %f or %g, and %% for a %)
Exit status 1 for a bad number, a zero increment or bad options.
